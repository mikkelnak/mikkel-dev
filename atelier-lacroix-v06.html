A.02 Statement 02 / 06
Manifesto
Issued 2026 · Rev. 04

A house in the mountains is, before anything else, an instrument for measuring weather. We draw long winter eaves, deep stone plinths, and rooms that hold their warmth into the late blue hour — then quietly let the morning back in.

A.03 Detail Section · Wall Assembly

Detail
holds weather.

Each wall is a small climate of its own — larch board, ventilated cavity, wood-fibre, cross-lam, lime plaster. We draft it at 1:5, then again at 1:1, until the snow line meets the sill exactly where we wanted it.

Every project leaves the atelier with a binder of three hundred details. Not because the house is complicated, but because the weather is.

Rooms tuned
to altitude.

We design as if the rooms were instruments and the mountain were a slow tide. Sleeping rooms sit east, drawing the first warm light off the Piz Languard. The salon faces south-west, where the valley exhales its evening warmth.

Ceilings are low where breath should gather, and tall where the larch wants to climb. Every threshold is a small altitude change — and every altitude change is a small instruction about how to slow down.

A.06 Studio Atelier Lacroix

A small atelier,
drawing slowly.

Marie-Jeanne Lacroix founded the atelier in 2008, after twelve winters at the Engadin valley's older offices. Today, six architects and two carpenters work between Sils-Maria and Zürich — drafting in graphite first, then in light.

We work on roughly four houses each year, never more. Every project is led by one architect, and every architect is, eventually, on site in February.
