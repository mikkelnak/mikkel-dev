Chalets Cut From Light

A Swiss studio for residential architecture above the treeline
— Manifest 001
We design houses that hold the alpine year — buildings tuned to slow snow, long shadow, and the particular hush of valleys at 1,800 metres. Each chalet begins as a quiet response to its slope, its weather, and the family who will return to it across decades.
— 02 / Practice

Detail Holds Weather

Larch boards laid with a 3mm drip. Copper flashings folded by hand in Samedan. We design the seams a building will live by — the line where rain runs, where ice rests, where the south sun finds the stone on a February morning.

Every junction is drawn three times: in summer, in storm, and in twenty years of patina.

— 03 / Interior

Rooms Tuned To Altitude

At elevation, daylight is harder and the air is thinner. We work with lower ceilings near the hearth, taller volumes at the glass, and a palette of grey-warm oak, lime plaster, and wool that reads gently against snow-light.

The result is a house that breathes slower than the valley below.

— 07 / The Studio

A practice held between two valleys.

Atelier Lacroix was founded by Elise Lacroix in 2014, after a decade with Peter Zumthor's office. The studio works from a converted hay-barn in Sils Maria and a small drawing room in Zürich, with seven architects and one resident dog.

We accept three private commissions a year.
