§ 00 — Premise

We design houses that hold the weather as gently as they hold their guests — measured in winters, drawn in graphite, finished in oiled spruce and dressed stone.

Studio brief, recorded in the Sils office, January 2026. Translated from the original German.
i.

Detail Holds Weather— a working note

Every joint at Atelier Lacroix is drafted twice: once for the eye, once for the föhn. The wind off the Bernina rounds corners we did not intend to soften — so we soften them on purpose, in oiled larch, in lead flashing, in chamfers a thumb wide.

We measure rainfall in millimetres and snow in metres. The roof line is not a gesture; it is an argument with the sky, settled in our favour by the time the first frost arrives.

A chalet, properly drawn, does not resist the season. It catalogues it.

Rooms
Tuned To

At 1,800 metres the air is thinner, the light is sharper, the floor wants to be warmer. We tune ceiling heights, window depths and stove placement to the precise altimetry of each site.

§ 03 — Studio

A small office,
made of winters.

Atelier Lacroix is twelve architects, two model-makers and one cat, working between a converted post house in Sils-Maria and a third-floor studio off Limmatquai in Zürich.

We accept four commissions per year. We draw by hand first. We do not photograph our work before its second snowfall.
